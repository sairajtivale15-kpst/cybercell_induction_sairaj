# Types of Phishing Attacks

## 1. Introduction

Phishing is a cyberattack in which attackers pretend to be trusted individuals, companies, or organizations to trick people into sharing confidential information. Different types of phishing attacks use email, text messages, phone calls, websites, and social media to target victims.

## 2. Email Phishing

Email phishing is one of the most common types of phishing attacks. Attackers send fraudulent emails that appear to come from legitimate organizations, such as banks, online shopping platforms, or educational institutions.

Example: A person receives an email claiming that their bank account will be blocked unless they verify their account details.

Prevention: Verify the sender's email address and avoid clicking suspicious links or downloading unexpected attachments.

## 3. Spear Phishing

Spear phishing is a targeted attack directed at a specific person or organization. Attackers may use the victim's name, workplace, college, or other publicly available information to make the message appear genuine.

Example: A college student receives a fake email that appears to come from their professor, asking them to open an unfamiliar document.

Prevention: Verify unusual requests directly with the sender through a trusted communication channel.

## 4. Whaling

Whaling is a type of spear phishing that targets senior executives, company directors, or other high-ranking individuals. These attacks often aim to obtain confidential business information or authorize fraudulent financial transactions.

Example: A company director receives a fake legal notice requesting confidential company documents.

Prevention: Verify sensitive requests and follow proper approval procedures before sharing information or transferring money.

## 5. Smishing (SMS Phishing)

Smishing is phishing carried out through SMS or text messages. Attackers send messages containing suspicious links or requests for personal information.

Example: A person receives a fake delivery message claiming that a parcel is on hold and asking them to pay an additional fee through a link.

Prevention: Avoid clicking unexpected links in text messages. Check delivery information through the courier's official website or application.

## 6. Vishing (Voice Phishing)

Vishing is a phishing attack conducted through phone calls or voice messages. Attackers may pretend to be bank employees, customer support agents, or government officials.

Example: A caller falsely claims to be a bank representative and asks for an OTP or banking password.

Prevention: Never share OTPs, passwords, or PINs over the phone. End suspicious calls and contact the organization using its official number.

## 7. Clone Phishing

Clone phishing involves creating a fraudulent copy of a legitimate email or message. The attacker modifies the message by replacing a genuine link or attachment with a malicious one.

Example: A person receives a copied version of a previously received document-sharing email, but the new attachment is unsafe.

Prevention: Check unexpected replacement links and attachments with the original sender before opening them.

## 8. Search Engine Phishing

In search engine phishing, attackers create fraudulent websites or advertisements that appear in search results. Victims may visit these websites believing they are legitimate services.

Example: A user clicks a fake banking advertisement in search results and enters their login credentials on a fraudulent website.

Prevention: Check the website's domain carefully and use official bookmarks or verified applications to access important accounts.

## 9. Social Media Phishing

Social media phishing uses fake profiles, fraudulent messages, or deceptive posts to trick users into revealing personal information or visiting malicious websites.

Example: A user receives a direct message claiming they have won a prize and must enter their account details to claim it.

Prevention: Verify profiles, avoid suspicious offers, and never enter sensitive information on unfamiliar websites.

## 10. Pharming

Pharming is an attack that redirects users from a legitimate website to a fraudulent one. It may involve manipulating DNS settings or other parts of the process used to locate websites.

Example: A user attempts to visit a banking website but is redirected to a fake page designed to steal login credentials.

Prevention: Use trusted networks, keep devices and routers updated, and pay attention to browser security warnings.

## 11. Business Email Compromise (BEC)

Business Email Compromise is a form of cybercrime in which attackers impersonate executives, employees, suppliers, or business partners to trick organizations into transferring money or disclosing sensitive information.

Example: An employee receives a fraudulent email that appears to come from a manager requesting an urgent payment to a new bank account.

Prevention: Confirm payment requests and changes to bank details through an independent, trusted communication channel.

## 12. QR Code Phishing (Quishing)

QR code phishing, also called quishing, uses malicious QR codes to direct users to fraudulent websites or unsafe actions.

Example: A fake QR code on a poster claims to offer a discount but redirects users to a fraudulent login page.

Prevention: Preview the destination URL before opening it and avoid entering sensitive information on unfamiliar websites.

## 13. Conclusion

Phishing attacks can occur through many communication channels and can target individuals as well as organizations. Understanding the different types of phishing helps users recognize suspicious activities and take appropriate precautions.

Practicing good cybersecurity habits, verifying the identity of senders, using multi-factor authentication, and avoiding suspicious links can significantly reduce the risk of phishing attacks.
